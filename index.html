What actually works

We don't sell miracles. Every ingredient shows its classification and the state of the research. Even when the result is sobering.

Magnesium
Contributes to normal muscle function and to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue. For night cramps in older adults the Cochrane review found no meaningful benefit, and that is part of the truth. For a confirmed deficiency, magnesium also exists as a licensed medicine.

You can't see it from the outside, and advertising is not a measuring device. A blood value at your GP brings the most clarity, ferritin or vitamin D for example. After that you know whether a supplement is worth it, which dose fits and how to measure success later. And any pharmacy will advise you free of charge.

Sometimes the difference is real, sometimes it isn't. A higher price can mean a better-tolerated compound like citrate instead of oxide, a verified dose, or a licence as a medicine. If none of that is behind it, the cheaper product is fine. That's why prices from 25 pharmacies sit side by side here, so you see at a glance where your product is cheapest right now.

A licensed medicine has an officially approved area of use and may say what it's for. A food supplement adds nutrients to your diet and may not treat illness. A dietary food for special medical purposes is made for the dietary management of specific conditions. The classification sits visibly on every product, so you know what you're buying.
